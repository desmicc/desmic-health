03 · THE APPOINTMENT

His doctor sees moments. He lives everything in between.

A clinician asks about sleep, medication, symptoms and possible events. Both are working with incomplete information. The missing interval is not the fault of either person.

04 · THE INFORMATION GAP

One person. Many disconnected pieces.

Clinical reports, symptoms, medication, sleep, movement and context may contain complementary information. The challenge is to align them, retain provenance and distinguish correlation from causation.

05 · A POSSIBLE FUTURE

What if we could see more of what happens between visits?

Imagine the same morning with an affordable wearable and a patient-controlled journal. This is a proposed research system, not an existing validated medical device.

06 · CONCEPT HARDWARE

Every measurement is a window. Not the whole picture.

Motion, optical pulse, skin temperature and optional skin conductance are candidate signals. Fall detection is a priority; seizure assistance requires task-specific validation. Wrist sensors do not directly measure brain activity.

The housing, battery and power budget affect size, usability and uptime.

07 · HOLISTIC PHYSIOLOGICAL INTELLIGENCE

The body does not operate as independent parts.

The central idea is a continuously evolving, uncertainty-aware personal model: neurological, autonomic, cardiovascular, sleep, behavior, medication, clinical history and environmental context.

Clinical history, reported events and suitable reference EEG; not brain measurements from the wrist.

08 · THE RESEARCH LOOP

Measure. Compare. Validate. Learn. Repeat.

Synchronize wearable signals with appropriate ECG, EEG/video-EEG, sleep studies, laboratory or clinical references. Assess signal quality, train on one group, validate on others, and revise hardware with evidence.

Synthetic, unaligned traces. Calibration requires appropriate clinical reference measurements, independent validation and version control.

09 · COST-OPTIMIZED OBSERVABILITY

How little must we measure to understand enough?

For a defined task, can lower-cost observations preserve useful information from expensive measurements? Some information is redundant; some is fundamentally unavailable. This is a research hypothesis, not MRI or EEG replacement.

10 · A POSSIBLE BETTER CONVERSATION

A better-informed conversation about the same human being.

Aarav and his clinician could review notes, synthetic observations, possible events, signal quality and missing intervals. Aarav controls access. The clinician retains responsibility for care.

11 · ACCESSIBILITY FIRST

Different lives. Different needs. The same dignity.

We envision affordable research and support for children, older adults, neurodivergent people, caregivers and people with chronic conditions: fall alerts, sleep, mental health, seizure assistance and accessible tools. Each requires separate evidence.

12 · DESMIC / A RESEARCH VISION

More freedom to live.

The future of healthcare should give us more freedom to live, not more reasons to think about being ill. Technology is a means, not the destination.

A research vision, not a clinical claim.

Original project vision and research ideas: Desmic (Kumar Shashank). Aarav is fictional; scene imagery is AI-generated and illustrative, not a testimonial. The proposed wearable, clinical workflows and models are conceptual. All displayed traces and model results are synthetic. No health data are collected.

Evidence for wearable seizure detection is limited to selected seizure types and devices; it does not establish universal prediction. No MRI, EEG or laboratory replacement is asserted. Patient consent, age-appropriate design, clinical review, uncertainty, and independent validation are essential.

Starting sources: ILAE/IFCN seizure detection guideline · SeizeIT2 research dataset · Wearable microneedle research.

Research experiment: 360 invented participants, 240 for training and 120 held out, standardized ridge regression for all 64 measurement subsets. The constructed tasks and arbitrary costs demonstrate a principle, not real clinical estimates. The displayed held-out frontier is descriptive, not independent model selection. Clinical calibration traces are illustrative, not fitted clinical models.
