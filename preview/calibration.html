SYNTHETIC ENGINEERING DEMONSTRATION

Measure. Compare.
Validate. Repeat.

A wearable signal is not the same thing as a clinical reference. This experiment illustrates how an inexpensive sensor may be calibrated for a narrowly defined measurement, how quality filtering changes coverage, and why evaluation must use different people.

All observations, units and outcomes below are invented. This is not ECG, EEG, or a validated medical algorithm. It makes no claim about seizure detection or clinical accuracy.

01 / Synchronize and inspect

The blue-green trace is a fictional reference; the warm trace is an observation from a simulated wearable. Change delay, noise and missingness, then compare the two traces.

03 / Decide what to improve

Observed limitationResearch response
Clock mismatchSynchronize acquisitions and verify drift.
Noisy measurementsImprove sensor placement, hardware or signal processing.
Missing dataReport coverage; evaluate real-world adherence and quality.
Performance on unseen peopleValidate independently and examine age, condition and population differences.

Real clinical research requires task-specific reference instruments, consent, safety and subgroup analysis, prospective validation, and expert review before any clinical use. A successful synthetic calibration is not evidence of real medical benefit.
